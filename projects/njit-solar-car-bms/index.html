NJIT Solar Car Team — BMS Team Lead; Staged Bench Validation

Staged Bring-Up for a Solar Car BMS

I led Orion BMS 2 integration and staged bench bring-up from initial CAN communication through one- and four-cell testing.

RoleBMS Team Lead
BMSOrion BMS 2
CellsMolicel P45B

Context

Taking responsibility in a rebuilding team.

The Solar Car effort was being rebuilt when I joined. I became BMS Team Lead in a five-person team context and took responsibility for moving the BMS work forward while learning the domain.

System work

Integration, telemetry, and thermal context.

The work used an Orion BMS 2 and Molicel P45B cells. I did most of the BMS programming; teammates contributed documentation and testing. The system work included CAN telemetry toward the CCU, intended load-disconnect/protection behavior, cooling-fan PWM, and precharge control. I also used Simscape Battery Builder for thermal-characteristic work.

Staged bring-up

Build the test in controlled steps.

  1. CAN communication
  2. Empty signals
  3. One cell
  4. Four cells
  5. Low-power / no-signal behavior
  6. Isolate power-source issue
  7. Portable 12 V DC supply
  8. Continue testing

During bring-up, a low-current power source produced low-power or no-signal behavior. The staged approach made the fault easier to isolate: replacing it with a portable 12 V DC supply resolved the immediate power issue and allowed testing to continue.

What I learned

Incremental testing makes faults easier to isolate.

Starting with CAN communication and adding cells incrementally made faults easier to isolate. Working through the power-source issue reinforced the value of controlled bring-up and disciplined documentation when learning an unfamiliar battery-management system.

Current status

Testing reached the controlled one- and four-cell bench stages. The complete battery pack had not yet been built at that stage.
